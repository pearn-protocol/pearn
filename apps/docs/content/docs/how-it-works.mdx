## Actors

| Actor | Does |
|---|---|
| Creator | Anyone. Picks the question, the close time and a resolver |
| Bettor | Bets stNEAR on YES or NO, claims principal plus winnings |
| Sponsor | Adds stNEAR to a market's pot. Gets it back only if nobody bets |
| Resolver | Set per market, ideally a DAO. Reports the outcome after close |
| Guardian | Can only turn an outcome into `Invalid`, and only during the 24 h challenge window |
| Keeper | Anyone. Calls `oracle.sync()`; ours runs every 12 h |

## Lifecycle

<Steps>
<Step>
### Create
`createMarket(question, closeTime, resolver)`. The close is at least an hour away; the question is at most 280 bytes.
</Step>
<Step>
### Bet
`bet(id, side, amount)` takes stNEAR. Your **principal** is fixed in NEAR at the current rate, and your **weight** is principal × seconds until close. Bets need an oracle reading younger than 2 days.
</Step>
<Step>
### Resolve
After close, once the oracle has synced again, the resolver calls `resolve(id, Yes | No | Invalid)`. That fixes the settlement rate and the pot. If nobody resolves within 7 days, anyone can call `voidExpired(id)`.
</Step>
<Step>
### Challenge window
For 24 h the guardian may call `overrideInvalid(id)`. It changes who shares the pot, never its size.
</Step>
<Step>
### Claim
`claim(id)` pays your principal back in stNEAR at the settlement rate, plus your share of the pot. See [payouts](/docs/payouts/).
</Step>
</Steps>

## Why time weight

Without it, a whale could bet on the obvious side just before close and take the yield that early bettors produced. With weight = principal × time left, a late bet earns almost nothing.
